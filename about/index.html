The short story

I kept watching good copy die in the handoff.

I started as a content writer. Blog posts, service pages and website copy, mostly for agencies and the healthcare, senior-living and software companies they served.

The writing was rarely the problem. The problem came after. The doc went to someone else, and the page that came back buried the headline, split the argument and put the next step where nobody would find it.

So I learned the parts I kept losing. First structure and editing, then layout and type, then enough frontend code to build the page myself. Now the words and the page come from the same desk.

— AK.

House rules

  1. 1Every sentence earns its place.
  2. 2Make the problem sharp, the answer believable and the next step obvious.
  3. 3A page should read like a plan, not a brainstorm.
  4. 4Respect the reader’s attention. Cut the noise.

Learned from Jason Fried and the Basecamp habit of plain talk.

AI Didn’t Kill Creativity. It Made Taste More Valuable.

Got a rough brief? Good. That’s where I start.

  • I ask who’s reading.

    And what they need to do next. Those answers shape everything after.

  • I explain every change.

    If I move a section or cut a line, you’ll know why, and we decide together.

  • I own the call.

    Tools can speed up a draft. The sources, the edits and the decisions are checked by me.

  • I finish the handoff.

    The work fits the page, the brief and the people who’ll use it.
