Why Everyone Sounds the Same Online

Online writing converges when useful conventions replace decisions. Purposeful specificity, not forced novelty, brings the voice back.

By Ahsan Khan

In this article

  1. Sameness is a social achievement
  2. Clarity and conformity are different
  3. Where the defaults enter
  4. A specificity audit
  5. Voice is accumulated judgment
  6. Evidence at a glance

The short version

  • Sameness started before AI: people adapt to their groups, copy what visibly succeeds and choose what feels safe.
  • Conventions that help the reader are worth keeping. The trouble starts when a convention replaces a decision.
  • Voice comes from accountable choices: what belongs to this subject, this reader and these stakes.

Open a few polished articles or posts from the same professional corner of the internet. The topics change. The rhythm often doesn’t.

A surprising confession opens the piece. A short sentence creates tension. Three lessons arrive in neat succession. The ending turns the argument into a broad principle about growth, leadership or change.

Any one piece may be competent. Read several together and the authors begin to disappear.

This is easy to blame on generative AI, and AI can certainly accelerate the effect, especially when many people accept similar suggestions, structures and turns of phrase. Yet the pull toward sameness started earlier. People learn the language of groups, notice what appears to succeed and repeat choices that feel legible and safe.

The real problem is larger than any tool. It is what happens when a useful convention replaces a decision.

Sameness is a social achievement

Adapting to others is part of communicating. People adjust their language and other signals toward or away from the people they’re talking to,1 and newer members of online communities learn the local norms as they take part.2

That adaptation serves a purpose. Shared language makes a person easier to understand. It signals competence, belonging and awareness of the conversation already under way. A field needs some common vocabulary, or every exchange would begin with a dictionary.

Visible success adds another pull. In a large experimental music market, showing people what others had chosen changed which songs they picked.3 That was music, not writing, but it shows visible popularity can shape choices beyond the qualities of the work itself. Creators also build theories about what platforms reward: one study of Instagram influencers described visibility as a game negotiated among users, community beliefs and algorithms, where algorithms structure behavior without determining it.4

A writer doesn’t need accurate knowledge of a ranking system to adapt around it. A repeated claim that a certain opening “works” can be enough. The proven hook becomes lower risk. The familiar structure is easier to approve. Consensus language attracts fewer objections. Each choice is understandable. Together, they narrow the field.

Generative tools add speed and scale. In one experiment, GPT-4 ideas improved evaluations of short stories, especially for writers who started lower, and the assisted stories also became more similar to one another.5 AI can improve an individual piece while reducing variety across a collection. In another experiment, ChatGPT cut completion time by 40% and raised evaluated quality by 18% on bounded professional writing tasks,6 which challenges the idea that AI assistance automatically produces worse work.

The issue is not assistance. It is unexamined acceptance.

Clarity and conformity are different

A campaign against sameness can quickly become a campaign against clarity. That would be a mistake.

Familiar forms help readers. People use their knowledge of textual forms to predict and organize what they read,7 and recurring phrases can carry real value: in academic writing they contribute to fluency, coherence and each discipline’s distinct voice.8 A checklist may be exactly right for a procedure. A standard phrase may name a concept precisely. A familiar opening may orient a reader without wasting time.

  • Clarity

    Reduces avoidable effort while preserving the complexity a subject requires.

  • Conformity

    Avoids choosing what this particular subject requires.

The distinction rests on function. If a convention helps the reader follow the argument, keep it. If it stays only because it was in the template, question it.

Forced novelty is no fix. Needless complexity in wording lowered readers’ judgments of an author’s intelligence.9 Replacing plain words with ornate ones may make a piece less familiar, but it also makes the reader work harder for nothing.

Distinctive writing does not need to be strange. It needs to be accountable.

Where the defaults enter

Sameness rarely arrives as one obvious sentence. It enters through a series of small choices.

  • Framing

    A borrowed premise, such as “the biggest mistake nobody talks about”, arrives before anyone checks that the subject hides a mistake at all.

  • Language

    “In this case” appears with no contrast to another case. “Transformation” stands in for a description of what changed.

  • Structure

    The argument is pressed into three lessons because the format expects three, though the second depends on the first and the third belongs elsewhere.

  • Examples

    A generic illustration confirms the claim without testing it. Swap in another industry and nothing meaningful changes.

  • Posture

    The writer hides inside accepted statements: consistency matters, change is hard, trust is important. True, perhaps, but agreement is not a reason to keep reading.

A specificity audit

Before trying to make a draft more original, ask whether its important choices can be explained.

A few of these in practice. “Consistency improves results” travels to any topic; “check the top two centimetres of soil before watering” belongs to one task. A three-step checklist works when the reader needs a sequence, and distorts the piece when the subject holds one hard trade-off that deserves sustained attention. “This matters more than ever” says little; a concrete consequence gives a claim weight without theatre. And a study of short stories can support a point about that experiment, but it can’t establish that all AI-assisted writing converges.

Voice is accumulated judgment

A recognizable voice is sometimes treated as decoration: sharper adjectives, unusual punctuation, deliberate informality. Those choices can become templates too.

Voice emerges more reliably from repeated acts of judgment. What does the writer notice? Which distinction do they protect? What evidence changes their mind? Where do they refuse an easy conclusion? Which details earn space, and which familiar lines get cut?

None of that means rejecting every convention. Good writing depends on shared forms. It doesn’t mean avoiding AI, templates or editing tools either: each can reduce effort and improve a draft. The responsibility stays with the writer. A tool can offer ten openings. A template can suggest an order. A visible success can reveal what readers understood. None of them can decide why a particular choice belongs to this subject, for this reader, under these conditions.

Sameness grows when those decisions are delegated by default. Voice grows when they are made on purpose, then made again.

Try it on one workflow

Audit one piece. Replace one default.

  1. Pick one recent piece and find a phrase, structure or claim you kept by default.
  2. Run it through the seven checks.
  3. Replace it with a choice grounded in this subject and this reader.

A specificity audit

Seven checks

CheckAskRevision move
☐ Subject
Does it belong here?		Could this sentence survive unchanged if the topic changed?Name the mechanism, object, condition or decision that belongs to this subject.
☐ Reader
Who is it for?		What does this reader need to notice, decide or do next?Keep conventions that shorten that path. Cut what only shows familiarity with a format.
☐ Stakes
Why does it matter?		What becomes different if the claim is true?Replace generic urgency with the actual consequence.
☐ Evidence
How do you know?		Is the statement measured, observed, inferred or illustrative?Label it honestly. An invented example can clarify; it cannot pose as proof.
☐ Structure
Why this shape?		Does the format reveal the argument’s dependencies?Move sections around. If the logic doesn’t change, the structure may be decoration.
☐ Language
What is this phrase doing?		Does the familiar phrase perform a needed job?Keep it if it does. Frequency alone doesn’t decide; function does.
☐ Posture
What will you defend?		What claim is the draft prepared to defend, and what limit does it admit?Trade consensus polish for a careful, bounded claim.
