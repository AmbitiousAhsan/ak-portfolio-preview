Egg cream

Milk, chocolate syrup and a hard blast of seltzer, stirred until a white foam rises to the top. There’s no egg in it, and no cream.

The story

The egg cream was the drink of New York’s soda fountains and corner candy stores in the first half of the 20th century, especially in Brooklyn and on the Lower East Side. It cost a few cents and needed a seltzer tap to make properly.

The name is a mystery. One theory says it comes from the Yiddish echt, “genuine”, cream; another says early versions really did have egg and cream in them. Either way, today’s recipe has neither.

Ways people make it

  • Chocolate, the classic
  • Vanilla, with vanilla syrup
  • With the syrup stirred in last, for a darker foam

How it’s made

  1. 1Syrup firstAbout two tablespoons of chocolate syrup in the bottom of a tall glass.
  2. 2Then cold milkAbout a third of the glass.
  3. 3Seltzer, hardSpray it in fast against a spoon until the foam reaches the rim. Stir at the bottom only, so the head stays white, and drink it straight away.
