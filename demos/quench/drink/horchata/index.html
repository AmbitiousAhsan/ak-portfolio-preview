Horchata

Rice soaked overnight with cinnamon, blended, strained and sweetened. Creamy and cold, no dairy needed.

The story

Horchata is one of Mexico’s aguas frescas, the cool, lightly sweet drinks ladled from big glass jars at markets and taquerías. This one is made from rice soaked with cinnamon, blended and strained into something milky without any milk.

The name and the idea travelled from Spain, where horchata is made from tiger nuts. Mexico swapped in rice and kept the cinnamon.

Ways people make it

  • With milk or evaporated milk for extra body
  • Horchata de chufa in Spain, made from tiger nuts

How it’s made

  1. 1Soak overnightRice and cinnamon sticks in water, left overnight.
  2. 2Blend and strainBlend until smooth, then strain through a fine sieve or cloth.
  3. 3Sweeten and chillStir in sugar and a little vanilla. Serve over ice with ground cinnamon.
