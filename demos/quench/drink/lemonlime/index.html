Lemon, lime & bitters

Lemonade, a good splash of lime cordial and a few dashes of aromatic bitters over ice. The pub order for whoever’s driving.

the room was the fun part. here’s the reading part ↓Draft entry · facts are being checked

The story

Order a soft drink at an Australian pub and there’s a good chance it’s this: tall, pink, fizzy, sweet and a little bitter, and often one of the cheapest things on the menu.

“Lemonade” here means clear lemon-lime soda, not the cloudy kind. The bitters do contain alcohol, but a few dashes in a tall glass of soda leaves only a trace, which is why it’s known as the designated driver’s drink.

Ways people make it

  • The pub way, with cordial
  • With fresh lime juice instead
  • Extra pink, with more bitters

How it’s made

  1. 1Bitters firstFour or five dashes into a tall glass, swirled around to coat it. That’s where the pink comes from.
  2. 2Ice and cordialFill the glass with ice and add a good splash of lime cordial.
  3. 3Top with lemonadeClear lemon-lime soda (what Australians call lemonade) to the top, and a lemon wedge on the rim.
