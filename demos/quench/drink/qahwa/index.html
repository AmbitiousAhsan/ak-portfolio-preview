Arabic coffee

Pale, spiced with cardamom and poured a little at a time into cups the size of a thimble. Across the Arabian Peninsula, it’s how you say welcome.

The story

Arabic coffee is less a drink than a ritual of hospitality. A host pours it for guests from a dallah, a brass pot with a long, curved spout, into small handleless cups called finjan. The beans are roasted lightly, so the coffee comes out golden rather than black, and it’s brewed with cardamom, sometimes with saffron or cloves.

Cups are filled only partway and refilled until the guest gently shakes the cup from side to side, the polite way to say “that’s enough, thank you.” In 2015, UNESCO added Arabic coffee to its list of the world’s intangible cultural heritage.

Ways people make it

  • Light and golden, heavy on cardamom
  • With saffron for colour and a floral edge
  • With cloves or a drop of rosewater
  • Darker roast, as you’ll find further north in the Levant

Goes with: Dates

How it’s made

  1. 1Roast lightlyGreen beans in a pan, just until they turn a pale cinnamon colour.
  2. 2Grind with cardamomCoarse coffee and crushed green cardamom. Saffron if it’s a special day.
  3. 3Simmer in the dallahBring it up slowly and let the grounds settle. Never let it boil hard.
  4. 4Pour a little, oftenA few sips per cup, dates on the side, refills until the cup is shaken.
