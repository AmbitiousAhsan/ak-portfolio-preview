Cedarline Care: process documents

The brief, sitemap, homepage outline and keyword map behind the Cedarline Care website. Cedarline Care is a fictional practice; the documents are the real working method.

1 · Content brief

Client: Cedarline Care (fictional): primary and urgent care practice

Problem: A single “book now” button hides several different care needs, so requests can land in the wrong scheduling queue.

Audience

  • Busy adults choosing a regular primary care team for prevention, follow-ups and ongoing concerns.
  • Adults with a new, non-emergency concern who are comparing a sooner visit with waiting for a routine appointment.
  • Patients with a test or follow-up in progress who need to know who explains the result and what happens next.

Goals

  • Separate the ongoing relationship (primary and preventive care) from a new, non-emergency concern on the first screen.
  • Make visit preparation and cost questions visible before anyone requests a visit.
  • Name who owns the next step after a visit, especially test results, and don’t count a portal notification as follow-up.
  • Carry each social post into a relevant guide, care page and the sample visit planner.

Voice

RuleDoDon’t
Name the visit, not a brand promise“A regular doctor. Room for the unexpected.”“Comprehensive healthcare for your busy life.”
Write actions in the visitor’s own words“A new concern has come up”“Book now.”
Keep urgent care free of speed promises“A route for non-emergency concerns that need attention sooner than a routine visit.”“Seen in minutes, guaranteed.”
Tell the reader what to confirm instead of asserting clinic facts“Confirm that the location can evaluate your concern before relying on a visit slot.”“We treat everything. Just walk in.”

Questions the site must answer

  1. Should I book primary care, a preventive visit or urgent care?
  2. Can I see the same care team for follow-ups?
  3. Does urgent care replace a regular doctor?
  4. Is every part of a wellness visit covered?
  5. What should I bring to a first visit with a new care team?
  6. Will a result appear before someone explains it, and who follows up?
  7. Can this location see my concern, and what should I do in an emergency?

Constraints

  • Fictional brand: an original concept with no operating practice, staff, appointments or prices. ZoomCare services was a public research reference, not a client, and no affiliation is implied.
  • No outcome claims: no results, recovery times, “best” or guaranteed language. Pages say what a visit may involve and what to ask, never what care will achieve.
  • No patient data online: the planner runs on fictional presets and collects no names, symptoms, photos or uploads. Sensitive care interests never go to analytics labels, URLs or advertising audiences.
  • No invented operating facts: clinicians, credentials, fees, accepted plans, hours and availability are left for the real practice to supply. Where the copy needs one, it says “confirm with the practice”.
  • Accessibility: target WCAG 2.1 AA (text contrast, keyboard-operable menu and planner, descriptive alt text, a skip link and plain-language headings). Prototype checks are not a certification.
  • Not a triage tool: the site cannot assess symptoms or urgency, and the homepage says to call 911 for a medical emergency in the U.S.

Success measures Proposed

  • Proposed, not measured: service page → planner starts and planner completion, using approved aggregate instrumentation after a real launch.
  • Proposed, not measured: staff-confirmed routing corrections (requests that arrive in the wrong scheduling queue), counted by the scheduling team.
  • Proposed, not measured: share of ordered tests with a named results owner at the visit, reported by the clinical team.

Deliverables

  • 24-page website: homepage, care overview, four care pages (Primary care, Urgent care, Preventive visits, Tests & follow-up), three guide pages, guides & offers, costs & coverage, people & approach, a locations overview and two location-concept pages (Market Street and Cedar Park), sample visit planner, proposed care handoffs, social campaign hub, four social profile previews, about this concept and demo privacy
  • Four social profile previews: Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and TikTok (local mockups, no live accounts or engagement counts)
  • Three ungated, downloadable guides: “Your first visit, without the scramble.” (New patient pack); “Same clinic. Different kinds of visits.” (Care-navigation guide); “The appointment is over. What happens next?” (Follow-up checklist)
  • Four-step sample visit planner using fictional presets, ending in a “next step has an owner” handoff summary
  • Proposed care-handoff page with a six-stage workflow from visit reason to billing
  • Portfolio case study with the evidence trail: decision, test, hypothesis, proposed metric and handoff rule
  • Flagship article: “Urgent care, the ER or your regular doctor? Where to go”
