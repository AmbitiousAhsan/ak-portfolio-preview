Goodkind Dental: process documents

The brief, sitemap, homepage outline and keyword map behind the Goodkind Dental website. Goodkind Dental is a fictional practice; the documents are the real working method.

1 · Content brief

Client: Goodkind Dental (fictional): general and cosmetic dentistry practice

Problem: Offers and smile photography can overshadow treatment choices, dental anxiety and the total cost.

Audience

  • Adults who have put off routine dental care and want to come back without being judged.
  • People with a new dental concern who need to know the appropriate next step.
  • Adults researching elective smile treatment who want suitability, limits and a written estimate before deciding.

Goals

  • Connect a friendly first impression to visit preparation and comfort preferences.
  • Put the written plan and cost conversation before the treatment decision.
  • Keep routine care, a new concern and an elective smile goal on separate routes with different expectations.
  • Carry each social post into a relevant guide, care page and the sample visit planner, with no countdown offers.

Voice

RuleDoDon’t
Give routine care and comfort the same weight as the smile“Good care. Clear choices. Your kind of dentist.”“Your dream smile starts with us.”
Never shame a gap in care“You don’t need a perfect explanation to come back.”“Don’t let neglect ruin your smile.”
No painless or price promises“You can discuss comfort, cost and the order of care before agreeing to a plan.”“Pain-free dentistry at unbeatable prices.”
Make the cost conversation explicit“A treatment plan should include a cost conversation.”“Affordable care for everyone!”

Questions the site must answer

  1. What if it has been years since my last visit?
  2. What happens at a first assessment?
  3. Can I discuss comfort needs before treatment?
  4. Do I have to agree to the whole treatment plan at once?
  5. What will I owe, and what should a written estimate include?
  6. Will my plan cover cosmetic treatment?
  7. Can I rely on an online slot for an urgent dental concern?

Constraints

  • Fictional brand: an original concept with no operating practice, staff, appointments or prices. MINT dentistry was a public research reference, not a client, and no affiliation is implied.
  • No outcome claims: no results, recovery times, “best” or guaranteed language. Pages say what a visit may involve and what to ask, never what care will achieve.
  • No patient data online: the planner runs on fictional presets and collects no names, symptoms, photos or uploads. Sensitive care interests never go to analytics labels, URLs or advertising audiences.
  • No invented operating facts: clinicians, credentials, fees, accepted plans, hours and availability are left for the real practice to supply. Where the copy needs one, it says “confirm with the practice”.
  • Accessibility: target WCAG 2.1 AA (text contrast, keyboard-operable menu and planner, descriptive alt text, a skip link and plain-language headings). Prototype checks are not a certification.
  • No promotional offers, membership plans or financing terms without real eligibility, exclusions and commercial approval. Recall timing follows the clinician’s plan, and the site cannot assess a dental concern’s urgency (the homepage says to call 911 for a medical emergency in the U.S.).

Success measures Proposed

  • Proposed, not measured: treatment-plan acknowledgement (patient confirms they received and understood the written plan), in aggregate after launch. No implication that every plan should convert.
  • Proposed, not measured: unresolved billing questions after an estimate, logged by the patient coordinator.
  • Proposed, not measured: completion of clinician-directed recall, through approved patient communications only.

Deliverables

  • 24-page website: homepage, care overview, four care pages (General dentistry, Restorative care, Cosmetic consultations, A dental concern), three guide pages, guides & offers, costs & coverage, people & approach, a locations overview and two location-concept pages (Goodkind Central and The Neighborhood Practice), sample visit planner, proposed care handoffs, social campaign hub, four social profile previews, about this concept and demo privacy
  • Four social profile previews: Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and TikTok (local mockups, no live accounts or engagement counts)
  • Three ungated, downloadable guides: “Coming back after a while?” (No-pressure first-visit pack); “Before you say yes to a treatment plan.” (Written-estimate checklist); “Bring a smile goal. Take home better questions.” (Cosmetic consultation planner)
  • Four-step sample visit planner using fictional presets, ending in a “next step has an owner” handoff summary
  • Proposed care-handoff page with a six-stage workflow from visit reason to billing
  • Portfolio case study with the evidence trail: decision, test, hypothesis, proposed metric and handoff rule
  • Flagship article: “How often do you need dental X-rays? Safety, timing and the apron”
