Juniper Women’s Health: process documents

The brief, sitemap, homepage outline and keyword map behind the Juniper Women’s Health website. Juniper Women’s Health is a fictional practice; the documents are the real working method.

1 · Content brief

Client: Juniper Women’s Health (fictional): OB-GYN and women’s health practice

Problem: Age-based assumptions and pregnancy-heavy messaging can make visitors with other questions feel out of place.

Audience

  • People preparing for a first or routine gynecology visit or a specific concern, including anyone for whom the visit feels unfamiliar or sensitive.
  • People with pregnancy or planning questions who are comparing care models or transferring care.
  • People who want a dedicated conversation about perimenopause or menopause.

Goals

  • Give gynecology, pregnancy and menopause care equal room in navigation and on the homepage.
  • Make comfort, access and communication preferences something to discuss before the visit.
  • Explain clinician roles and keep “result released”, “clinician reviewed” and “follow-up completed” distinct.
  • Carry each social post into a relevant guide, care page and the sample visit planner, without using sensitive interests for targeting.

Voice

RuleDoDon’t
Start with the question, not a life stage“Start where you are.”“Exceptional care through every stage of womanhood.”
Don’t assume pregnancy, age, relationship or identity“Pregnancy care is one path here, not the whole story.”“Ready to start your family? Book today.”
Make consent and comfort explicit“You can discuss the process, preferences and consent before proceeding.”“The exam only takes a few minutes, so there’s nothing to worry about.”
Leave diagnosis to clinicians“You do not need a diagnosis to prepare for a conversation.”“Hot flashes? It’s menopause, and here’s what fixes it.”

Questions the site must answer

  1. Can I book a routine visit or discuss a concern without knowing the name of a service?
  2. Will a first visit always include an examination?
  3. How do I choose between pregnancy care models?
  4. Do I need to know what is causing my symptoms before a menopause conversation?
  5. How do I ask for an interpreter, a support person or another access preference?
  6. Can I book a scan directly, and who reviews the result?
  7. Where do results, cost and referral questions go?

Constraints

  • Fictional brand: an original concept with no operating practice, staff, appointments or prices. Women’s Healthcare Associates was a public research reference, not a client, and no affiliation is implied.
  • No outcome claims: no results, recovery times, “best” or guaranteed language. Pages say what a visit may involve and what to ask, never what care will achieve.
  • No patient data online: the planner runs on fictional presets and collects no names, symptoms, photos or uploads. Sensitive care interests never go to analytics labels, URLs or advertising audiences.
  • No invented operating facts: clinicians, credentials, fees, accepted plans, hours and availability are left for the real practice to supply. Where the copy needs one, it says “confirm with the practice”.
  • Accessibility: target WCAG 2.1 AA (text contrast, keyboard-operable menu and planner, descriptive alt text, a skip link and plain-language headings). Prototype checks are not a certification.
  • Sensitive care topics (pregnancy, menopause, gynecology concerns) stay out of page URLs that could be shared with ad platforms, analytics event labels, social lead forms and advertiser audiences. Keyword work is organic only.

Success measures Proposed

  • Proposed, not measured: in voluntary usability sessions, whether participants find menopause care or a first gynecology visit as easily as pregnancy care.
  • Proposed, not measured: approved aggregate navigation measures (route and guide use), with no sensitive-interest audiences built from them.
  • Proposed, not measured: unresolved scheduling, coverage and referral questions logged by coordinators, reported in aggregate.

Deliverables

  • 24-page website: homepage, care overview, four care pages (Gynecology, Pregnancy care, Menopause conversations, Imaging coordination), three guide pages, guides & offers, costs & coverage, people & approach, a locations overview and two location-concept pages (Juniper House and Eastbank Rooms), sample visit planner, proposed care handoffs, social campaign hub, four social profile previews, about this concept and demo privacy
  • Four social profile previews: Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and TikTok (local mockups, no live accounts or engagement counts)
  • Three ungated, downloadable guides: “A first visit, on your terms.” (Visit comfort planner); “Before you choose a pregnancy care team.” (Care-model question pack); “Make room for the changes you’re noticing.” (Menopause conversation guide)
  • Four-step sample visit planner using fictional presets, ending in a “next step has an owner” handoff summary
  • Proposed care-handoff page with a six-stage workflow from visit reason to billing
  • Portfolio case study with the evidence trail: decision, test, hypothesis, proposed metric and handoff rule
  • Flagship article: “Perimenopause vs. menopause: signs and when to talk to someone”
