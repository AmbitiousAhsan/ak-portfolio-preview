Kitewell: process documents

The brief, sitemap, homepage outline and keyword map behind the Kitewell website. Kitewell is a fictional practice; the documents are the real working method.

1 · Content brief

Client: Kitewell (fictional): pediatric ABA and autism support clinic

Problem: Warm reassurance alone leaves parents to ask the real questions themselves: what care involves, whether their child will be heard, whether it fits the week and what it might cost.

Audience

  • Parents exploring whether ABA support may fit their child, who need a plain explanation of the service before anything else.
  • Families who have been referred or are comparing providers, and need practical information on the approach, team, location, costs and the route into intake.
  • Referring offices that need their own route and a clear acknowledgement of what happens to a referral.

Goals

  • Explain care before asking for an inquiry.
  • Make child comfort, costs and family routines easy to find.
  • Carry the same useful content into four social channels.
  • Show the next owner of each inquiry without implying a live clinical integration.

Voice

RuleDoDon’t
Name the question under the reassurance“What might your family pay? Start with a clear estimate.”“Let’s make room for the practical questions.”
Name the person and the role, not just “the team”“Who will work with your child? Know the person and the role.”“Different people. A shared understanding of the next step.”
Welcome difficult questions“What if my child doesn’t want to? That deserves a clear answer before care begins.”“Kids love coming here!”
Don’t promise fit or outcomes“ABA is one way to support new skills; whether it fits your child is a conversation with a clinician.”“Proven results for every child.”

Questions the site must answer

  1. What happens in care: an assessment, a session and a review?
  2. Will my child be heard, and what happens if they need a break or don’t want to take part?
  3. Can this fit our week, including school and family routines?
  4. What might our family pay, and what does a benefits review actually tell us?
  5. Does checking insurance confirm an appointment?
  6. Who will work with my child, and who supervises the care?
  7. We’ve been referred. What does intake need next, and what are we waiting for?

Constraints

  • Fictional brand: Kitewell is an independent portfolio concept with no operating clinic, commissioned client or clinical review. Cortica, Hopebridge and Action Behavior Centers were public references only.
  • No outcome claims: no promised progress, “proven” results, recommended weekly hours or universal fit. Care sequences are labelled illustrative.
  • No patient data online: the sample inquiry runs on fictional presets; no diagnosis, plan details or records are asked for on a public form, and marketing sees totals only.
  • No invented operating facts: staff, credentials, ages served, hours, wait times, fees, accepted plans and insurer logos are left for a real clinic to supply.
  • Sourced context only: general care and cost explanations link to AAP, BACB, HealthCare.gov and CMS, and need clinical review before real use.
  • Accessibility: target WCAG 2.1 AA (keyboard-operated tabs, carousels and filters, reduced-motion support, descriptive alt text). Prototype checks are not a certification.

Success measures Proposed

  • Proposed, not measured: in moderated parent sessions, the share of participants who can explain the proposed service after reading the care page.
  • Proposed, not measured: inquiries that reach the right owner (intake, billing or clinical) without rerouting, from aggregate intake counts after launch.
  • Proposed, not measured: post-to-guide paths, meaning aggregate clicks from a social post to the page that answers its question.

Deliverables

  • 25-page clinic website: home, about, care, getting started, first-conversation questions, insurance and costs, benefits and approval questions, care-team roles, our spaces, visit guide, family resources, locations overview and two location-concept pages (Northside: assessment openings; Riverbend: waitlist), referrals for professional offices, contact with a sample inquiry, social hub with four profile previews, research notes, proposed handoffs, case study and demo privacy
  • Two views of every page: Parent, and Clinic team (a page brief and numbered notes on each decision)
  • A seven-stop guided tour for clinic owners and hiring managers
  • Four local social profile previews: Instagram (topic filters), Facebook, LinkedIn and TikTok (captioned storyboards)
  • A selectable question pack plus blank cost-summary and weekly-planning worksheets
  • A four-step sample inquiry with topic-specific acknowledgements and an intake queue preview showing the next owner
  • An operational explorer of proposed marketing → intake → care → billing handoffs
  • Portfolio case study
  • Flagship article: “Does insurance cover ABA therapy in Texas? A guide for families”

2A good place to begin: Where are you right now?Route families by where they are (exploring, referred or with practical questions) so nobody has to start with all the answers.Three route buttons (I’m exploring ABA · We’ve been referred · I have practical questions) with panels: Understand the care, Understand the next step and Make the details clearer, each linking to care, getting started, the sample inquiry, insurance or spaces. A separate link for referring offices.
3How we think about care: There’s a whole person behind every little moment.Explain the care approach in everyday terms before any request, and say plainly that fit is a clinician conversation.A paragraph on assessment, family-relevant goals and ongoing conversation; the line that ABA is one way to support new skills and fit is decided with a clinician; three principles (Listen before planning · Make goals meaningful · Keep checking in); link to the care page.
4A little look inside: Before the first visit, make it a little familiar.Help a family picture the space and prepare a child, and turn each room into a question worth asking.A four-stop space tour (A warm hello · Room to explore · A moment to pause · Time together), each with features and “A question to bring”, labelled as AI-generated illustrative spaces; link to all spaces.
5The first visit, in small steps: Nothing to rehearse. A few things to know.Lower the stakes of a first visit and set the expectation that the family leaves with an agreed next step.Four steps (Say hello · Get familiar · Take a moment · Know what’s next), a note that every child’s experience differs, and a link to the first-visit guide.
6Insurance & the practical details: Let’s make the paperwork part clearer.Bring the cost question forward and separate what is confirmed from what is pending.A short explanation (start with plan, service and location; ask what is confirmed, pending and what costs may apply) and a link to insurance and costs.
7Find your place: Two settings. The same thoughtful welcome.Show that each location has a different next step, so a waitlist isn’t a surprise.Two location concepts: Northside (assessment openings expected; sample route assessment inquiry, next contact intake review) and Riverbend (waitlist; sample route waitlist inquiry, next contact availability update).
8For the questions in between: The little Kitewell guide.Offer preparation tools that answer the practical and people questions before a call.Make-your-visit-pack feature plus three guides: Four questions to start with (first conversation) · What does a benefits review actually tell you? · Who is with my child? Who supervises the care. Link to all family resources.
9The Kitewell everyday: A little of our world. Wherever you find us.Show that social content continues the same useful answers, with a distinct job per platform.Tiles for Instagram (save a guide, bring a question), Facebook (care and your week), LinkedIn (clear handoffs) and TikTok (visual stories), with the note that profiles are fictional, with no live accounts or engagement claims.
10On your mind: Good questions belong here. / Your next step: Start with the question you have.Answer the last objections in place, then close with one low-pressure action and a path through the site.Five FAQs (Is ABA the right fit for every child? · Can we ask questions before beginning? · What if my child needs a break? · Does checking insurance confirm an appointment? · Can I book a real appointment here?), then the CTA “Talk with intake” and a five-step parent path (Get to know Kitewell → Understand the care → Sort out the practical side → Prepare for a visit → Start a conversation).
