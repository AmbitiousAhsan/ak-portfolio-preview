Reframe Orthopedics: process documents

The brief, sitemap, homepage outline and keyword map behind the Reframe Orthopedics website. Reframe Orthopedics is a fictional practice; the documents are the real working method.

1 · Content brief

Client: Reframe Orthopedics (fictional): orthopedics and physical therapy practice

Problem: A long specialty list makes the patient do the routing work, so therapy referrals and existing plans get pushed through the new-patient funnel.

Audience

  • Adults with a joint or movement concern deciding whether to see an orthopedic specialist.
  • Patients with a therapy referral or an existing rehabilitation plan who need the physical therapy route, not a new consultation.
  • People who have discussed surgery and want to understand the options, logistics and recovery handoff.

Goals

  • Give a first orthopedic consultation and an existing physical therapy plan separate entry points.
  • Make referral, records and imaging requirements clear before a request.
  • Keep the whole recovery in view: specialist, therapy and follow-up owners connect without any promise of a particular outcome.
  • Carry each social post into a relevant guide, care page and the sample visit planner.

Voice

RuleDoDon’t
Start with the activity, not the anatomy“A painful shoulder. A knee that slows the stairs.”“Advanced orthopedic solutions for every lifestyle.”
Never promise a recovery outcome or timeline“Ask how the plan will be set, reviewed and adjusted.”“Pain-free in six weeks.”
Route by the visitor’s situation“I’m arranging physical therapy”“Book now.”
Treat a consultation as a conversation, not a commitment“A consultation is a place to understand the options and ask questions.”“Get your surgery scheduled fast.”

Questions the site must answer

  1. Should I request a specialist consultation, physical therapy or an injury visit?
  2. Do I need a referral or new imaging first?
  3. What happens at the first orthopedic appointment?
  4. How many therapy visits will I need, and how is the plan reviewed?
  5. Is a consultation a commitment to surgery?
  6. Can every injury be seen here?
  7. Who coordinates the next step if therapy or a procedure is recommended?

Constraints

  • Fictional brand: an original concept with no operating practice, staff, appointments or prices. OrthoVirginia was a public research reference, not a client, and no affiliation is implied.
  • No outcome claims: no results, recovery times, “best” or guaranteed language. Pages say what a visit may involve and what to ask, never what care will achieve.
  • No patient data online: the planner runs on fictional presets and collects no names, symptoms, photos or uploads. Sensitive care interests never go to analytics labels, URLs or advertising audiences.
  • No invented operating facts: clinicians, credentials, fees, accepted plans, hours and availability are left for the real practice to supply. Where the copy needs one, it says “confirm with the practice”.
  • Accessibility: target WCAG 2.1 AA (text contrast, keyboard-operable menu and planner, descriptive alt text, a skip link and plain-language headings). Prototype checks are not a certification.
  • No exercise instruction or return-to-activity advice in social content; videos show the administrative route, not treatment.

Success measures Proposed

  • Proposed, not measured: referral completeness at receipt (share arriving with the requested records), once operational definitions exist.
  • Proposed, not measured: manual rerouting between the consultation and therapy routes, counted by the referral coordinator.
  • Proposed, not measured: time from referral receipt to review, reported in aggregate after a real launch.

Deliverables

  • 24-page website: homepage, care overview, four care pages (Orthopedic consultation, Physical therapy, Injury visits, Surgical planning), three guide pages, guides & offers, costs & coverage, people & approach, a locations overview and two location-concept pages (The Movement Center and Westside Studio), sample visit planner, proposed care handoffs, social campaign hub, four social profile previews, about this concept and demo privacy
  • Four social profile previews: Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and TikTok (local mockups, no live accounts or engagement counts)
  • Three ungated, downloadable guides: “What do you want to get back to?” (First-visit planner); “Your first therapy visit, unpacked.” (Rehab preparation pack); “Help the next team pick up the thread.” (Referral handoff guide)
  • Four-step sample visit planner using fictional presets, ending in a “next step has an owner” handoff summary
  • Proposed care-handoff page with a six-stage workflow from visit reason to billing
  • Portfolio case study with the evidence trail: decision, test, hypothesis, proposed metric and handoff rule
  • Flagship article: “Do I need a referral for physical therapy or an orthopedic doctor?”
