Solenne Skin: process documents

The brief, sitemap, homepage outline and keyword map behind the Solenne Skin website. Solenne Skin is a fictional practice; the documents are the real working method.

1 · Content brief

Client: Solenne Skin (fictional): medical and cosmetic dermatology practice

Problem: Beauty-led messaging can bury medical care, while clinical menus leave cosmetic decisions unexplained.

Audience

  • People who want a clinician to evaluate a skin change, discomfort or an ongoing skin condition.
  • Adults researching an optional cosmetic treatment who want suitability, risks and cost before they decide.
  • Existing patients reviewing a clinician-led care plan or a follow-up.

Goals

  • Give a medical concern and a cosmetic goal their own route from the first screen.
  • Let a medical visitor reach care without passing through cosmetic messaging.
  • Show risks, alternatives and the written estimate for cosmetic treatment, with no countdowns or discounts.
  • Carry each social post into a relevant guide, care page and the sample visit planner.

Voice

RuleDoDon’t
Make room for uncertainty“Your skin. Your questions. A clearer next step.”“Reveal your best skin with expert care.”
No pressure in cosmetic copy“A practical question list with no countdown, discount or pressure.”“Book this week and save 20%.”
Educate without fear“A change deserves a conversation.”“That mole could be deadly. Book now.”
Leave clinical judgment to the clinician“Only an individual clinical assessment can answer that.”“Most spots are harmless, so don’t worry.”

Questions the site must answer

  1. Is my question a medical concern or a cosmetic goal, and where do I start?
  2. What happens at a skin-check visit?
  3. Will I need a biopsy?
  4. Can I send a photo for a diagnosis?
  5. Does a cosmetic consultation mean treatment that day?
  6. What might insurance cover, and can I get a written estimate for elective care?
  7. Should I change my treatment based on a social post?

Constraints

  • Fictional brand: an original concept with no operating practice, staff, appointments or prices. Forefront Dermatology was a public research reference, not a client, and no affiliation is implied.
  • No outcome claims: no results, recovery times, “best” or guaranteed language. Pages say what a visit may involve and what to ask, never what care will achieve.
  • No patient data online: the planner runs on fictional presets and collects no names, symptoms, photos or uploads. Sensitive care interests never go to analytics labels, URLs or advertising audiences.
  • No invented operating facts: clinicians, credentials, fees, accepted plans, hours and availability are left for the real practice to supply. Where the copy needs one, it says “confirm with the practice”.
  • Accessibility: target WCAG 2.1 AA (text contrast, keyboard-operable menu and planner, descriptive alt text, a skip link and plain-language headings). Prototype checks are not a certification.
  • No photos or skin images collected anywhere (the copy explains that a real practice must provide an approved clinical route), and no before-and-after transformations or fear-based cancer messaging.

Success measures Proposed

  • Proposed, not measured: route-specific guide use (medical vs cosmetic guides), in aggregate after a clinically reviewed launch.
  • Proposed, not measured: qualified consultation requests, meaning requests that arrive in the correct medical or elective queue.
  • Proposed, not measured: staff-reported mismatches and cost questions raised after a written estimate. No treatment-result metric is claimed.

Deliverables

  • 24-page website: homepage, care overview, four care pages (Medical dermatology, Skin-check visits, Cosmetic consultation, Ongoing skin care), three guide pages, guides & offers, costs & coverage, people & approach, a locations overview and two location-concept pages (The Garden Practice and City Rooms), sample visit planner, proposed care handoffs, social campaign hub, four social profile previews, about this concept and demo privacy
  • Four social profile previews: Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and TikTok (local mockups, no live accounts or engagement counts)
  • Three ungated, downloadable guides: “A skin concern or a cosmetic goal?” (Choose your conversation); “Before you say yes to a treatment.” (Cosmetic consultation planner); “Bring the question. Leave the filter.” (Medical visit preparation)
  • Four-step sample visit planner using fictional presets, ending in a “next step has an owner” handoff summary
  • Proposed care-handoff page with a six-stage workflow from visit reason to billing
  • Portfolio case study with the evidence trail: decision, test, hypothesis, proposed metric and handoff rule
  • Flagship article: “Medical vs. cosmetic dermatology: what insurance usually covers”
