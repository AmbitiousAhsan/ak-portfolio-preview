Does insurance cover ABA therapy in Texas? A guide for families

In Texas, whether ABA is covered depends mostly on what kind of plan your child has. State law requires many state-regulated health plans to cover ABA for a child diagnosed with autism before age 10. Texas Medicaid covers ABA for eligible members under 21. But most Texans with a health plan have a self-funded plan, which follows its own plan documents instead of the state rule. Here's how to tell which one applies to you, and what to ask next.

Structure: Step 1: Find out what kind of plan you have → Step 2: If your plan is state-regulated → Step 3: If your child has Texas Medicaid → Coverage isn't the same as a yes → Questions to ask your plan or benefits team → Coverage is one question. Fit is another.

3 · Patient guide

Kitewell · first conversation

Brief: four prompts a family can bring to any clinic, so the first call is useful whoever they choose.

01 · What are we hoping to understand? Write down the questions you want answered. You might be exploring whether the service fits, what the assessment involves or how the clinic works with families. You do not need to arrive with a treatment plan.

02 · What matters in our everyday life? Think about the routines, interests and practical concerns you would like to discuss. Your observations give the clinician context; they do not need to be translated into clinical terminology.

03 · What would make attending practical? Consider location, transport, realistic scheduling windows and any access or communication needs. Ask which details the team needs before arranging a visit.

04 · What happens when we finish this conversation? Ask who owns the next step, what information is outstanding and how you can follow up. An inquiry is the beginning of a review, not a confirmed assessment.

4 · Social captions

Kitewell · Instagram concept

Brief: saveable posts that answer one question each and lead to the page with the full answer.

WHAT TO EXPECT Inside an ABA assessment.

An ABA assessment and an autism diagnostic evaluation serve different purposes. An ABA assessment informs a proposed behavior-analytic care plan. This example shows four parts to discuss before attending: your perspective, observation, the findings and the proposed next step. The clinician determines the actual assessment.

PREPARING FOR A VISIT Before you arrive: the small details.

Knowing the entrance is useful. Knowing who will greet you, where you will wait and how to request a pause is useful too. These are practical details to confirm before a visit. The images show Kitewell’s fictional space concept; access arrangements must be confirmed with the actual clinic.

INSURANCE & COSTS What might our family pay?

An insurer’s name on a website is not an individual coverage decision. Before agreeing to care, request an explanation tied to the exact service, provider, location and plan. Ask about cost sharing, approval limits and charges outside covered care. Keep the estimate and the date of the review.

6 · An edit

Clinic service page section (~300 words) · 273 → 230 words

Brief: Reframe Orthopedics (fictional), physical therapy page: help a new or referred patient understand the first visit, check the referral question and request an assessment.

Draft (written for the demonstration)

Physical Therapy at Reframe At Reframe Orthopedics, we are pleased to be able to offer our patients a comprehensive and state-of-the-art range of physical therapy services. It is our belief that every patient deserves the very best care, and our team of highly skilled, experienced therapists is dedicated to providing exactly that. Our physical therapy program utilizes evidence-based modalities and a multidisciplinary approach to optimize functional outcomes across a wide variety of musculoskeletal conditions, including post-operative rehabilitation, sports injuries and chronic pain. Treatment plans are developed following an initial evaluation, and progress is monitored on an ongoing basis. With our proven techniques, most patients are back to their normal activities in just a few weeks, and many are able to avoid surgery altogether. We are the best physical therapy clinic in the area, and our results speak for themselves. Patients who are attending PT for the first time should arrive 15 minutes early. Edited

Physical therapy What do you want to get back to? Physical therapy at Reframe starts with that question, then builds a plan around it. Who it's for You're recovering from surgery or a sports injury, or living with long-term (chronic) pain, and you want a plan that fits your everyday life. You may already have a referral, or you may be starting with an assessment. Your first visit Your physical therapist asks about your goals and history, assesses how you move, and agrees a treatment plan with you. You'll review progress together along the way, and you'll leave with exercises to continue at home. Sessions usually last 45 to 60 minutes [confirm]. Before you come in - Ask your insurance plan whether you need a referral for physical therapy. - Bring your referral, if you have one, and any paperwork from your surgery. - Wear comfortable clothing you can move in. - Arrive 15 minutes early [confirm]. How long will therapy take? That depends on your assessment and your goals. Ask your physical therapist how your plan will be set, reviewed and adjusted. Insurance and costs We accept many major insurance plans [confirm the list before publishing]. Our front desk team can answer questions about your coverage. Request a physical therapy assessment New patients: request an assessment [link]. Already in a therapy plan? Log in to the patient portal [link].

Three of the ten reasons

  • The opening was throat-clearing with an unsupported “state-of-the-art” claim; leading with the patient’s goal gives the reader a reason to keep going.
  • Nothing here can be checked by a reader, and team credentials should be stated specifically and verified, or left out.
  • Clinical jargon is replaced with what actually happens at the visit, using the draft’s own facts (an initial evaluation, a plan, monitored progress).
