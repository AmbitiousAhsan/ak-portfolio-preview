The free first call, on paper

A real clinic’s website.
The first three things I’d change.

This is what you get from the free 30-minute call, done on a real independent clinic’s public website. The clinic isn’t named, and I’ve left out anything that would identify it. Everything below is something I saw on its pages.

Clinic
An independent physical therapy clinic in the San Antonio area
Specialty
Outpatient physical therapy, orthopedic and sports
Reviewed
November 2, 2026
Pages read
Homepage · Contact and appointment page · Patient reviews page · Neighborhood page · Resource links page

The first three, in order of what patients feel

  1. 01

    Make asking for an appointment obvious, and say what happens next.

    What I saw
    The homepage has several booking buttons, but the call button in the header doesn’t show the number. The appointment section of the contact page repeats the address and phone numbers and says nothing about who replies, or how soon.
    Why it matters
    A patient in pain who reaches out after hours wants to know someone will get back to them, and roughly when.
    The fix
    Put one short request form (name, phone, what hurts, best time to call) right under the appointment heading, add a line like “We’ll call you back within one business day”, and show the number beside every Call button.
  2. 02

    Lead with what hurts, not with treatment names.

    What I saw
    The main Services menu lists about 18 treatment techniques, several in clinical terms a patient wouldn’t search for. The homepage has a good “conditions we treat” section (back pain, sprains, dizziness), but it sits lower down and isn’t in the menu.
    Why it matters
    People look for “knee pain” or “after surgery”, not a technique, so a menu of methods makes them work to find out whether they’re in the right place.
    The fix
    Add a “What hurts?” item at the top of the menu, linking to the condition pages that already exist, and move the technique list underneath as “How we treat”.
  3. 03

    Rewrite the neighborhood page for people, not search engines.

    What I saw
    The page for the clinic’s own neighborhood repeats the same “physical therapy for [area]” phrase many times, names nearby towns twice, and has a heading with nothing under it.
    Why it matters
    This is often the first page a local patient lands on from Google. Repetitive filler makes a careful clinic look careless.
    The fix
    Three short, plain paragraphs: who you help, the conditions you see most, and how to get there and book, with the phone number and a booking button in the body.

Also worth fixing

  • Explain the two phone numbers. A second number has no label, and the main number says call or text without saying what texting is for. Label it, and add “Text us to book or reschedule.”
  • Make the review count match everywhere. The homepage and the reviews page give different counts, and the reviews list has near-duplicates. Show one current rating and count across the site.
  • Fix image descriptions left over from a template. Several image descriptions, the text screen readers read aloud, name clinics in other cities. Rewrite each to say what the image shows.
  • Pick one promise about how soon patients are seen. One section promises same-week appointments; another promises immediate ones. Use the one that’s true most weeks, everywhere.

What already works

  • Address, phone and hours are easy to find on every page, and early-morning openings suit people who work.
  • The homepage names the major insurance plans, says no referral is needed, and tells people to call if their plan isn’t listed.
  • Real reviews that name individual therapists, and a news section that’s kept up to date.

Every audit says what to keep, too. Fixing what’s broken shouldn’t break what’s working.
