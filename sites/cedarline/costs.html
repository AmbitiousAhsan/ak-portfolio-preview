Costs

Four bills that surprise people.

Primary and urgent care bills usually make sense once you know how the visit was billed. Ask these before you’re seen, then print your list.

01

Clinic owner note 1 These four are the surprises specific to primary + urgent care: visit-type billing, the check-up that becomes a problem visit, the outside lab bill and the freestanding ER. Each comes with the question that heads it off and who answers it.

Q01Bill surprise

Urgent care or office visit?

The same problem can be billed as an urgent care visit or as an office visit, and plans often treat the two differently.

Ask

Will this be billed as an urgent care visit or an office visit?

The practice’s billing team

Q02Bill surprise

The check-up that becomes a problem visit

A preventive visit is often covered differently from a visit about a new problem. Bring up something new at your check-up and part of it may be billed separately.

Ask

If I raise a new problem at my wellness visit, could part of it be billed separately?

The practice, then your plan

Q03Bill surprise

The lab bill that arrives later

Blood tests and swabs are often processed by an outside lab, which can send its own bill weeks after the visit.

Ask

Will my tests go to an outside lab, and is that lab in network for my plan?

The practice, then your plan

Q04Bill surprise

Urgent care, or an emergency room?

Some buildings with “emergency” on the sign are freestanding emergency rooms, billed as emergency care. Cedarline’s Door A is urgent care.

Ask

Is this an urgent care clinic or an emergency room, and how will the visit be billed?

The facility, before you’re seen

Also worth asking

  1. Q05

    Is this clinician, at this location, in network for my plan?

  2. Q06

    Do I need a referral for this visit?

  3. Q07

    If I’m paying myself, what does this type of visit cost before any tests?
