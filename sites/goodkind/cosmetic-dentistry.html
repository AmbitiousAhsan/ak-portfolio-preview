You’d like to change how your smile looks?

You are considering an elective change and want to understand options before deciding. Take your time. You can ask, compare and say no.

A good consultation

Five questions worth more than a photo.

Bring your goal in your own words. These questions help you see past a before-and-after picture to the decision itself.

Is it suitable right now?

Ask whether your teeth and gums are healthy enough for the change you have in mind, or whether something else comes first.

What will it really change?

Ask what the treatment can realistically do for your goal, and what it won’t. A good answer includes the limits.

What else could work?

Ask about alternatives, including smaller steps and doing nothing for now. You are allowed to choose less.

How do I look after it?

Ask how long it may last, what upkeep it needs and whether it may need replacing one day.

Can I see it in writing?

Ask for a written elective plan and estimate, and take it home. No countdown offer should hurry you.

How a consultation may go

Three steps, no rush.

  1. Explain what you would like to change.
  2. Discuss oral health, suitability, risks and alternatives with the dentist.
  3. Review the proposed steps, maintenance and written estimate.

Who does what

  1. The coordinator books a consultation
  2. the dentist checks suitability
  3. you take home a written elective plan

Bring with you

  • Your goal in your own words
  • Questions about durability and maintenance
  • Your budget questions, without assuming a quoted price

Will my plan cover cosmetic treatment?

Do not assume coverage. Ask the practice and your plan about the exact proposed services, and request the expected patient cost before committing.

What you won’t find here

No pressure, on purpose.

We’ll never tell you “Your dream smile starts with us.” A smile goal is yours to define, and it sits alongside your oral health, not ahead of it.

  • Before-and-after photos that promise a result
  • Countdown offers or “today only” prices
  • A “dream smile” headline that decides for you
  • Coverage promises for elective treatment
