This fictional website can’t assess symptoms or connect you to care. For a medical emergency in the U.S., call 911. For personal dental or health questions, contact a qualified care team.

2

For the clinic. The call script hands your front desk the facts it triages on: which tooth, since when, and what makes it worse.

What to say when you call

Three things, in your own words.

“Say what hurts, since when, and what makes it worse: hot, cold or biting.”

That’s enough to start. The front desk may also ask:

  • Which tooth or side, roughly
  • When it started, and whether it’s getting worse
  • Any swelling, and where
  • Whether a piece broke off or a filling came out
  • Any recent dental work in that spot

Can I rely on an online slot for urgent help?

Confirm the practice’s availability and scope directly. For a medical emergency, use emergency services. This sample booking flow does not connect you to care.

Bring

  • Relevant recent dental treatment details
  • Your medication list
  • Questions about the visit and likely cost components

From the call to the chair

What usually happens next.

The steps a practice may follow. A real dental team decides what an assessment involves.

  1. 1

    Call and describe it

    Say what hurts, since when, and what makes it worse: hot, cold or biting.

  2. 2

    Get the right appointment

    The front desk tells you whether the practice can see you, how soon, and what to do if it can’t.

  3. 3

    Get it checked

    The dentist looks at the area, explains what they find and talks through the options.

  4. 4

    Know what happens next

    Before you leave, ask about any follow-up visit and who to call if it changes.

Who does what

  1. The front desk books you
  2. the dentist checks it
  3. you leave knowing who calls you next
