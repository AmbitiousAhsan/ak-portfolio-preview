Understand the options

1

For the clinic. For the patient who walked out with a plan and didn’t book. The three ways a plan can go give your treatment coordinator a shared vocabulary before the follow-up call.

If a dentist has suggested work

Your plan, three ways.

A dentist has identified a concern and you want to compare the proposed options. Most plans can go more than one way, and which options fit you depends on an individual assessment.

Treat it now

Often discussed when a problem is likely to get worse, or is causing pain.

Ask

  • “What happens if I wait?”
  • “How many visits, and how long is each?”

Stage it

Splitting the plan across visits, or across plan years.

Ask

  • “Which part comes first, and why?”
  • “Does staging change the estimate?”

Watch and wait

Some findings can be checked again later instead of treated now.

Ask

  • “What would change your mind?”
  • “When should we look again?”
2

For the clinic. It links to the written-plan explainer, so the estimate conversation starts with the patient already knowing what each line means.

At the follow-up

Ask in this order.

  1. “What is this treatment meant to fix?”
  2. “What are the other options, including waiting?”
  3. “Can I have the plan and the estimate in writing?”

Do I have to agree to the whole plan at once?

Ask the dentist which parts are time-sensitive, how options compare and whether care can be staged. The answer depends on an individual assessment.
