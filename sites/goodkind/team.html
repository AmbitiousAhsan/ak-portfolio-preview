How we talk

Words first, tools later.

Meeting the team and the room before anything happens can take the edge off. Ask whether a talk-only first appointment is possible.

Coming back is the hard part. Saying this up front lets the team start from today, not from the years in between.

Knowing what comes next, and roughly how long it takes, turns a surprise into a plan.

Some people feel calmer when they can see what the dentist is describing. Others would rather not. Either is fine.

Staying in control

You can pause. You can ask.

Agreeing a signal before anything starts means you never have to talk with your mouth open.

Longer visits can include pauses. Ask how the dentist likes you to signal one.

Your dentist decides what is suitable for you. You can still ask what each option involves and what you will feel.

Settling in

Small things that make a room feel kinder.

A podcast or a playlist can soften the sounds of a visit. Ask whether headphones are fine for the step you are having.

Bright light straight above you is a common thing people mention. Bring your own or ask if the practice has a pair.

Some times of day are calmer than others. Ask the front desk when the waiting room tends to be quiet.

A friend or family member can sit with you. Ask the practice how it handles a support person in the room.

    Who reads your card

    Know who handles the next question.

    A clear explanation matters as much as a welcoming room. Explore the visit, ask about comfort and compare a written treatment plan at your own pace.

    Room to say what matters

    A good visit starts with listening.

    1. Tell the team what would help. Ask about access, interpretation, a support person or an explanation before each step.
    2. Ask for a clear explanation. Options, consent, costs and what needs deciding now or later.
    3. Know how to raise a concern. A practice should tell you who handles questions about care, communication and billing.
