Gynecology & everyday health

Gynecology. Your questions belong here.

A conversation about reproductive health, prevention or a concern you want taken seriously.

“Something’s changed, and I’d like to ask about it.”

01

For the clinic · note 1The landing page for a “first gynecology visit” ad. It answers the examination question before the call, one the front desk hears often.

You are preparing for a first visit, a routine conversation or a specific concern.

Before anything happens

  1. You talk first.

    The visit starts with what you’d like to ask about, not with an examination.

  2. You decide.

    If an examination is suggested, the clinician explains what it involves and why, and asks before starting.

  3. You can stop.

    Pause, slow down or stop at any point. You don’t have to give a reason.

02

For the clinic · note 2The three consent lines are clinical copy: your clinical lead signs off on every word before launch.

Questions you might have

You might askWill a first visit always include an examination?

Something to knowAsk the clinician what is recommended for your situation and why. You can discuss the process, preferences and consent before proceeding.

You might askCan I ask for a chaperone, or bring someone with me?

Something to knowYou can ask. Raise it when you book, so it can be arranged before the visit rather than in the moment.

You might askWhat if my question doesn’t feel “big enough”?

Something to knowIf it is on your mind, it is worth raising. You don’t need the right words or a diagnosis, only the question you most want answered.

03

For the clinic · note 3Each answer ends with something to ask, not a promise, so the page never commits the clinic to a policy it hasn’t confirmed.

If an examination is suggested

  1. Ask whether today needs an examination at all, or whether it can wait.

  2. Ask for a chaperone, or bring someone with you.

  3. Ask whether a smaller speculum or a different position is possible.

  4. Say if a past examination was difficult. You don’t have to explain why.

Worth bringing

  • Your questions and current medications
  • Relevant records requested by the practice
  • Comfort, access or communication preferences
