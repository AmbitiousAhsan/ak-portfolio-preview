How a first visit works

Three things you can always ask for.

  1. Step 1

    You talk first.

    Start with what you most want to ask. You don’t need the right words, or a name for what’s going on.

  2. Step 2

    Nothing happens without an explanation.

    If an examination is suggested, the clinician explains it and asks before starting. You can pause or stop.

  3. Step 3

    You leave with a plan.

    You know what happens next, who follows up, and how to reach them.

Questions

Asked before the first visit.

Still unsure? Start a card and bring the question with you.

Book a visit →
Will a first visit always include an examination?

Not always. Ask the clinician what is recommended for your situation and why. You can talk through the process, your preferences and consent before anything happens.

Can I ask for a chaperone?

Yes, you can ask. Mention it when you book, so it can be arranged before the visit rather than in the moment.

Am I too young to ask about perimenopause?

There is no minimum age. If changes in your periods, sleep, mood or energy are affecting your days, that is reason enough to ask.

Can I come with pelvic-floor questions after having a baby?

Yes, even if it has been a few years. Ask for a gynecology visit and say what has changed; you can add it to your conversation card.

How do I avoid a surprise bill?

Ask before the visit how it will be billed, and ask your plan what that costs you. The costs page lists the four moments worth asking about.
