Pregnancy & planning

Pregnancy care. Understand the path ahead.

Explore the questions involved in starting or transferring pregnancy care.

“I have pregnancy questions, whatever they turn out to be.”

Pregnant and bleeding, in severe pain, or your baby is moving less? Call your maternity unit now, or 911.

01

For the clinic · note 1The urgent line sits above everything else on this page. It needs clinical sign-off and your real maternity-unit number before launch.

You want to understand the care model, first appointment and how the team coordinates visits.

Starting, or moving your care here?

Starting pregnancy care

For a first appointment with the pregnancy team.

  • Ask when the first appointment is usually booked.
  • Ask which scans and tests are usually offered, and which are optional.
  • Bring your current medication and supplement list.

Moving your care here

For anyone transferring from another practice.

  • Ask how the team requests your records, and how long it takes.
  • Bring any notes from previous care you want to discuss.
  • Ask whether your next scheduled appointment can stay on track.
02

For the clinic · note 2New and transferring patients need different things from intake: transfers bring records. Splitting the paths here means the coordinator asks for the right documents on the first call.

Who might care for you

  • Obstetricians

    Doctors who specialize in pregnancy and birth.

  • Midwives

    Clinicians who care for you through pregnancy, often alongside a doctor.

  • Your coordinator

    Books visits, scans and referrals, and knows who to ask.

Ask where you would give birth, which hospital the team works with, and how your preferences are talked through along the way.

03

For the clinic · note 3Care roles are described, not named people, until verified profiles exist. The hospital question is one patients should ask; the page never claims hospital privileges.

Questions you might have

You might askHow do I choose between care models?

Something to knowAsk who you would see, where visits and the birth would happen, and how your preferences are discussed. Our pregnancy guide lists the questions side by side.

You might askCan I come in before I’ve made any decisions?

Something to knowPregnancy questions can come before any decision. Ask what a first conversation covers and who you would speak with.

You might askWho do I contact between appointments?

Something to knowAsk how to reach the clinical team for time-sensitive concerns and what happens outside office hours. For an emergency, call 911.
