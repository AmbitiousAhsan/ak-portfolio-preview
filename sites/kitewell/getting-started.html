01 / Your starting point

You don’t need to
arrive with the same story.

A diagnostic evaluation considers developmental or diagnostic questions. An ABA assessment helps inform a proposed ABA care plan. They are different processes.

Exploring developmental concerns

You do not have to decide on ABA to ask about the process. For developmental or diagnostic questions, start with your child’s pediatric clinician and discuss an appropriate evaluation route.

Kitewell’s concept does not provide autism diagnosis.Read about care and fit↗

Already have a diagnosis or referral

Clarify what the referring clinician recommended, which service is being considered and what the clinic and health plan require before an assessment.

A diagnosis or referral alone does not confirm coverage or availability.Explore the sample inquiry↗

Moving from another service

Discuss continuity with the clinicians involved. Ask about the new assessment process, existing goals, appropriate record sharing and any new plan requirements.

A previous schedule or authorization may not carry over to a new provider.Prepare for the conversation↗

02 / From a question to a possible assessment

Know who is doing what.

The exact documents and sequence depend on the service, clinic and plan. This is the proposed Kitewell process.

Explore the sample inquiry↗

  1. First conversation · intake

    Clarify the question, location preference and practical needs. Confirm whether the service is within scope and who will follow up.

  2. Required information · intake and billing

    Identify what is needed and why. This may include a referral, relevant reports or plan information. Ask for an approved way to share it; a missing document should have a named next action.

  3. Requirements and possible costs · billing

    Review the exact plan, service, provider and location. Explain any authorization requirement, cost estimate and unresolved questions.

  4. Assessment readiness · clinical team and scheduling

    Confirm the relevant requirements, suitable clinician availability and family preferences before agreeing a date. Acknowledging an inquiry does not book a visit.

  5. Findings and recommendations · clinician and family

    Discuss the assessment, options and any proposed plan. Treatment may involve further authorization or scheduling steps.

03 / Planning your week

Care is part of a life.
Plan for the whole week.

Ask for the proposed commitment in writing and discuss what is practical. An available time slot is not a clinical recommendation, and there is no universal number of hours in this concept.

Download the weekly-planning checklist↗

□Proposed session length and frequency, and the clinical reason for them.

□Caregiver meeting time, participation and anything suggested outside sessions.

□School, travel, work, other supports and time to rest.

□Who provides sessions and how staff changes are introduced.

□Illness, cancellation and missed-session policies, including any charges.

□How to raise a concern if the schedule or care is not working.

The checklist contains prompts only. It does not prescribe a care schedule.

04 / If there is a wait

Waiting for what,
and until when?

Ask whether the wait is for an assessment, ongoing care or both. An estimated wait is not a confirmed start date.

What determines a suitable opening?+

Ask how service needs, clinician availability, location and family scheduling preferences are considered. A place on a list does not by itself establish when care can begin.

What should the next update tell me?+

Agree the contact, communication route and next update point. An update should explain any change, what is still pending and whether you need to do anything.

Can we change preferences or explore other options?+

Ask how to update preferences and whether another appropriate location or route can be discussed. Talk with your child’s clinician about suitable alternatives while waiting.

What needs another check when an opening appears?+

The clinic may need to revisit current information, requirements, coverage and clinical fit. Confirm what is ready before treating an opening as an appointment.

The facts a real clinic must confirm.

Age range, clinician credentials, actual hours, access arrangements, plan participation and current availability must come from the operating clinic. These facts are not established for this fictional brand.
