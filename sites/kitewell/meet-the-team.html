Family guide / The people involved

Who will work with your child?
Know the person and the role.

An intake contact, a supervising clinician and the person providing a session have different responsibilities. Before starting, ask the clinic to put actual names and contact details against each role.

01

Clinical lead / BCBA

Assesses needs, explains behavior-analytic recommendations, oversees the plan and reviews care. A BCBA is a graduate-level certification; ask about the actual clinician’s qualifications and any applicable licensure.

Bring this person questions about

Goals, methods, progress, clinical concerns and changes to the plan.

“Who supervises my child’s care, and when can I speak with them?”
02

Direct support / technician

May deliver agreed activities under clinical supervision. An RBT is a certified paraprofessional who works under direction and close supervision, rather than independently.

Bring this person questions about

What happened during a session and how observations reach the supervising clinician.

“Who will work with my child, how are they supervised, and how are staff changes introduced?”
03

Intake and scheduling

Explains the entry process, identifies outstanding information and coordinates practical next steps. This role does not decide the clinical plan.

Bring this person questions about

Required information, location preferences, readiness and the next update.

“Who is our contact while we are getting started or waiting?”
04

Benefits and billing

Reviews plan information and requirements, explains estimates and handles billing questions. Coverage decisions remain with the health plan.

Bring this person questions about

Plan participation, authorizations, possible costs and differences on a bill.

“Who will explain what is confirmed, what is pending and what we may owe?”

Your part in care

Participation should
be explained, too.

Ask what involvement is expected and discuss what is practical for your family.

  • When you will meet with the supervising clinician.
  • What updates you can expect after sessions.
  • Whether caregiver meetings or activities outside visits are proposed.
  • How your observations and your child’s preferences affect reviews.
  • How to request a change or raise a concern.
