The Kitewell spaces

A place to arrive.
Room to get comfortable.

Explore the welcome area, play room, quiet corner and family space. These are design concepts for a clinic experience, not photographs of an operating location.

THE KITEWELL SPACE CONCEPT / 01
Come on in

A small welcome can make a big difference.

A place to arrive, put a bag down and ask where to go. The welcome desk is the starting point for the Kitewell space concept.

  • A visible place to check in
  • Seating alongside the welcome area
  • A clear introduction before moving on
A question to bring

“Can we see the entrance and arrival routine before our visit?”

THE KITEWELL SPACE CONCEPT / 02
Come on in

Follow the thing that catches their interest.

Books, building blocks and room to move. This play-room concept gives everyday interests a place in the conversation.

  • Child-height shelves and materials
  • Space for seated and floor activities
  • Room for a caregiver to join
A question to bring

“How will my child’s interests shape the assessment?”

THE KITEWELL SPACE CONCEPT / 03
Come on in

Sometimes, a little less is helpful.

A quieter corner with fewer things in view. A space concept for pausing, reading or taking a moment away from a busier room.

  • A simple, uncluttered layout
  • Soft seating and adjustable light
  • A place to discuss comfort and breaks
A question to bring

“How do you respond when my child needs a break?”

THE KITEWELL SPACE CONCEPT / 04
Come on in

Your perspective has a seat at the table.

An opportunity to share what you notice in daily life. A family conversation can cover what matters, what feels manageable and what still needs explaining.

  • Everyday routines as a starting point
  • Questions about the proposed goals
  • Time to agree the next conversation
A question to bring

“Who can I contact if a part of the plan is not working?”

Before you arrive

The details
are worth asking.

A lovely room is only part of the experience.

Before a real visit, confirm the practical details with the clinic. A space should work for your family, including any communication, mobility or sensory needs you want to discuss.

Arrival

Where do we park, and which entrance should we use?

Access

What accessibility arrangements are available at this location?

Comfort

Can we discuss lighting, noise, waiting and breaks beforehand?

Familiarity

Can you share photos or a visit sequence before we arrive?

Caregiver role

Where will I be during the visit, and when can we talk?
