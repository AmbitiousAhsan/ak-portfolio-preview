Case study / Research notes

Start with the decisions
a family needs to make.

This is desk research and a review of provider website structures. The audience segments below are design hypotheses, not findings from interviews or a clinic audit.

Who the site is designed for

Parents exploring support need a plain explanation of the service. Families comparing providers need practical information about the approach, team, location and costs. Families already referred need a clear route into intake. Referring offices need their own process and acknowledgement.

Care and participation

The American Academy of Pediatrics describes individualized, developmentally appropriate, family-centered supports and shared decision-making. This informed the emphasis on strengths, family priorities and questions to discuss, rather than universal promises.

AAP: considerations for supports and services ↗

Practical information

AAP’s general guidance on choosing a pediatrician identifies qualifications, communication, location, hours, insurance and coordination as questions families may consider. This is a general information principle; it is not ABA-specific consumer research.

HealthyChildren: choosing a pediatrician ↗

Provider website patterns

Cortica, Hopebridge and Action Behavior Centers were reviewed for the visibility of service information, locations, insurance guidance and getting-started routes. Their claims, service scope and outcomes are not transferred to Kitewell.

  • Cortica · services, locations and family/referral paths.
  • Cortica payment options · plan and location-specific coverage context.
  • Hopebridge · care explanations and family resources.
  • Action Behavior Centers · location discovery and a visible start-of-care route.

Operational scope

The separate workflow demonstration distinguishes eligibility, authorization, claim receipt, adjudication and payment. It does not establish vendor compatibility. Production requirements depend on the actual clinic, systems, service and plan.

CMS: adopted transaction standards ↗

Parent-focused content review

An editorial walkthrough identified gaps in concrete care examples, comfort and consent questions, team responsibilities, family time commitments, the distinction between diagnosis and an ABA assessment, and cost preparation. These are design-review findings, not parent-interview results. The site now provides illustrative sequences and generic worksheets, without inventing clinic facts or a recommended schedule.

  • BACB ethics code — understandable explanations, consent, involvement and ongoing review.
  • BACB: BCBA and RBT — distinct professional roles and supervision.
  • HealthCare.gov — deductible, copay and coinsurance context.

What to test next

  • Can families explain the proposed service after reading the care page?
  • Can they find the relevant location and understand its next step?
  • Can they distinguish an inquiry from an appointment?
  • Can referring offices identify the right route?
  • Does social content lead to the page that answers its question?
