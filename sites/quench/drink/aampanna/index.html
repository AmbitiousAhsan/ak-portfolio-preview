Aam panna

Unripe green mango, cooked and mashed into a sour-sweet, lightly spiced drink with cumin, black salt and mint. A summer cooler.

The story

Aam panna (आम पन्ना, also called aam ka panna) is a drink of unripe green mango that is cooked, mashed and mixed with water, a sweetener and spices. Saveur calls it a thirst-quenching north Indian drink.1 An Oxford University Press culinary textbook describes it as a refreshing summer drink.2

Why green mangoes

Unripe mangoes are tart and rich in pectin, and Indian cooks use them to add tang to everything from dals to desserts. The first of the season’s green mangoes are eagerly awaited in early spring.1

Boiled or roasted

The fruit is cooked first. Saveur’s recipe boils the mangoes until tender, then peels and pits them and purées the pulp.1 A Roli Books recipe also boils them until tender and pulpy, then scrapes the pulp off the stones and strains it.3 Other cooks roast instead. The textbook describes unripe mango roasted in a tandoor, and the author Nandita Haksar remembers that Lucknow’s aam ka panna tasted smoky because the mangoes were roasted on coals instead of boiled.2, 4

What goes in

The sweetener is sugar or jaggery. Saveur’s version adds black pepper, black salt, cardamom, cumin and asafoetida.1 The Roli book gives a sweet version with sugar, cardamom and saffron and a savory one with roasted ground cumin and salt.3 A peer-reviewed paper on sugar in India glosses aam panna as a beverage of raw mango, mint leaves, sugar and salt.5 Since the recipes differ, the balance of sour, sweet and salty is the cook’s call.

In the heat

Poonam Saxena’s Hindustan Times column rebuilds a 1950s north Indian summer in a Kanpur bungalow from her aunts’ memories. In the evenings, as the sun’s fury faded, cooling drinks were passed around: aam ka panna, bel ka sherbet, masala chhas and nimbu paani.6

Ways people make it

  • Boiled or roasted mangoes, depending on the cook
  • With jaggery instead of sugar
  • Sweet with cardamom and saffron, or savory with roasted cumin and salt

How it’s made

  1. 1Cook the mangoesBoil or roast whole raw mangoes until they are tender.
  2. 2Take the pulpPeel the mangoes and scoop the soft pulp away from the stone.
  3. 3BlendBlend the pulp with sugar or jaggery, roasted cumin, black salt and mint, then thin it with cold water.
  4. 4Chill and pourServe it cold, over ice.
