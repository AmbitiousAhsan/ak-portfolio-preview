Kenyan chai

Strong black tea simmered in a pot with about as much milk as water and a good amount of sugar. The breakfast cup, and the welcome.

The story

In Kenya, chai just means tea, and the usual cup is black tea with milk and plenty of sugar.3, 5 It doesn’t have to be spiced, unlike the “chai” on many café menus abroad.5 Writing for the Smithsonian Folklife Festival, Sabrina Lynn Motley described it as a drink of generous hospitality.1

How it’s made

It’s made in a pot on the stove. The Smithsonian’s version heats water and black tea leaves near boiling for four to six minutes, then adds milk and sugar for two to four more. Its recipe uses a cup of water to a cup of milk.1 The Standard, a Kenyan newspaper, gives the same split for a spiced version: water first, then ginger, cardamom and cloves for about five minutes, then the tea, then the milk.4

The tea is typically CTC (crush, tear, curl), small particles that brew fast and strong and keep their color against milk.5 Spicing varies by home. A Johannesburg engineer born in Nairobi told the Mail & Guardian that the chai at home leans on ginger and cloves, and that the more rural you go, the sweeter the tea gets.3

How it’s served

Chai is a breakfast staple, often with mandazi, a fried pastry like a doughnut, or with mahamri or bread.2, 4

A short history

Tea is a late arrival in Kenya. Settlers first planted it in 1903 at Limuru, as an experiment. Commercial growing began in 1924 under colonial settlers, and African farmers weren’t allowed to grow tea until 1956.6 Motley also calls tea’s history in Kenya complicated and ties it to colonialism.1 The Mail & Guardian traces Indian spices and cooking methods to Mombasa’s history as a spice-trade hub and notes that Kenyan and Indian cooking share a love of sweet chai.3

Ways people make it

  • Plain, with only tea, milk and sugar
  • Spiced, with ginger, cardamom and cloves
  • With mandazi or mahamri on the side at breakfast

  2. 2Add milk and sugarAbout as much milk as water, and sugar to taste.
  3. 3Keep it just under a boilA few more minutes on the heat, until the tea is dark and milky.
