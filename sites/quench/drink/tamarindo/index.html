Agua de tamarindo

Tamarind pods simmered, soaked and strained into a sweet-sour drink the color of milky caramel. One of Mexico’s most popular aguas frescas, with horchata, jamaica and limón.

The story

Agua de tamarindo is made all over Mexico, and Larousse’s dictionary of Mexican food lists it among the country’s most popular aguas frescas, with horchata, jamaica and limón.1 It’s brown, sweet and sour, made from the sticky pulp inside tamarind pods.

How it’s made

It can be made with raw or cooked tamarind. Raw has more flavor, Larousse says, but it can cause diarrhea, so cooked is more common. Either way the tamarind is soaked, the seeds come out and the pulp is blended with water.1

A recipe in the Mexico City daily Excélsior follows the familiar steps: crack off the shells, pull away the fibrous veins, simmer the pulp, sweeten it while it’s hot, then rub the pulp off the seeds by hand and top up with cold water.4 Many markets also sell balls of tamarind pulp mixed with sugar, ready to dissolve in water.1

How it’s served

Cold, with an optional slice of lime or a glass rimmed with salt and chile powder.4 Tamarind is also a common paleta flavor; Larousse notes that paletas are usually made from regional aguas frescas.6

Where tamarind comes from

Tamarind is native to tropical Africa and was grown in India so long ago that it has often been reported as native there too.2, 3 It reached tropical America early; Excélsior dates its arrival to the Spanish colonial period.3, 4 Morton lists Chiapas, Colima, Guerrero, Jalisco, Oaxaca and Veracruz as Mexico’s main growing states.3

Not just a drink

In Mexico tamarind goes into sweets as well as aguas frescas.2 Dulce de tamarindo, pulp mixed with sugar, salt and powdered chile piquín, is sold across the country, most often on the coasts.5

Ways people make it

  • Classic, sweet and sour
  • With a slice of lime, or a glass rimmed with salt and chile powder
  • Frozen into paletas

How it’s made

  1. 1Peel the podsCrack off the brittle shells and pull away the stringy veins.
  2. 2Simmer and soakSimmer the pulp in water for a few minutes, then leave it to soak until soft.
  3. 3Mash and strainSqueeze it with your hands to free the seeds, then strain.
  4. 4Sweeten and chillSugar to taste, more cold water and plenty of ice.
