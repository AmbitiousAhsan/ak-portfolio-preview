Pisco sour

pisco sour
Contains alcohol · adults of legal drinking age

Pisco, citrus and sugar, usually shaken with egg white and finished with drops of bitters. Peru and Chile both have living traditions of it, and both claim it.

Caipirinha

caipirinha
Contains alcohol · adults of legal drinking age

Cachaça, lime and sugar, muddled in the glass and filled with crushed ice. Brazilian law calls it the country’s typical drink.

  • Sugar · from your pantry

champurrado

Tepache

tepache

Pineapple peel fermented for a few days in water with piloncillo, sometimes with cinnamon and cloves. Sweet, a little sour, low in alcohol and sold from barrels by the cup or the bag.

agua de tamarindo

Tamarind pods simmered, soaked and strained into a sweet-sour drink the color of milky caramel. One of Mexico’s most popular aguas frescas, with horchata, jamaica and limón.

café de olla

Coffee simmered in a clay pot with piloncillo and cinnamon, then strained into a mug. Sweet, lightly spiced and made with water alone.

chicha morada

Purple corn simmered with pineapple, quince, cinnamon and cloves, then strained, sweetened and served over ice with a squeeze of lemon. Deep purple, sweet and a little tart.

Limonada de coco

limonada de coco

Coconut milk blended with lime juice, sugar and ice into a pale, frothy limeade. A coconut shake with, in most recipes, no dairy at all.

ponche navideño

Tejocotes, guavas, apples, sugarcane and prunes simmered with piloncillo and cinnamon, then ladled hot into mugs. The drink of the posadas.

Paraguay · Tea & infusions · Cold

Tereré

tereré

Yerba mate sipped through a metal straw with ice-cold water and, usually, a few crushed herbs. Paraguay’s way of staying cool, and its first entry on UNESCO’s intangible heritage list.

What’s in it
  • Refreshing herbs, such as mint · from the market
  • Ice and cold water · from your freezer
