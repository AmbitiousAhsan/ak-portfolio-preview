01

The brief

Before a word is written, we agree who is reading, what they came to find out and how we’ll know the piece worked.

Reader
Parents and caregivers in Austin and elsewhere in Texas who are exploring ABA for their child and are trying to make sense of their insurance or Medicaid coverage.
Why they’re here
A Texas parent or caregiver exploring ABA wants to know whether their child's plan is likely to cover it and what to check before an assessment.
Main search phrase
ABA therapy insurance coverage Texas
Also covers
Texas autism insurance mandate · Texas Medicaid ABA therapy · does insurance cover ABA therapy · self-funded plan autism coverage · ABA prior authorization Texas · ABA therapy Austin insurance
Done when
A parent can tell which rules apply to their child’s plan, and knows exactly what to ask next.
02

Questions and sources

I list the reader’s real questions, then find a primary source for every answer: the law, the regulator, the program itself.

The reader’s questions

  1. Does my child’s plan have to cover ABA at all?
  2. What if we’re on Texas Medicaid or CHIP?
  3. If it’s covered, why might we still be told no?
  4. What should I ask before the first assessment?

8 sources, all primary

03

The outline

One heading per question, in the order the reader needs them. You see and approve this before the draft.

  1. H2 · 1

    Step 1: Find out what kind of plan you have

    The one question that decides everything else, and how to answer it from an insurance card.

  2. H2 · 2

    Step 2: If your plan is state-regulated

    What the state mandate actually requires, rule by rule, including the age-10 rule and the cap.

  3. H2 · 3

    Step 3: If your child has Texas Medicaid

    The other big route, with the two requirements that shape the first steps.

  4. H2 · 4

    Coverage isn't the same as a yes

    Network, prior authorization and cost: the gap between “covered” and “approved”.

  5. H2 · 5

    Questions to ask your plan or benefits team

    A checklist a parent can take to the phone call.

  6. H2 · 6

    Coverage is one question. Fit is another.

    Ends on the family’s decision, not the sale.

04

The draft

The answer comes first. Every claim links to its source, and plain words replace jargon.

The opening, as published

In Texas, whether ABA is covered depends mostly on what kind of plan your child has. State law requires many state-regulated health plans to cover ABA for a child diagnosed with autism before age 10. Texas Medicaid covers ABA for eligible members under 21. But most Texans with a health plan have a self-funded plan, which follows its own plan documents instead of the state rule. Here's how to tell which one applies to you, and what to ask next.

Why it opens this way

  • The direct answer is in the first sentence: it depends on the plan type.
  • The two exceptions a parent most needs (Medicaid, self-funded plans) are named before they scroll.
  • The last line tells them what the rest of the article will do for them.
05

Review

Your expert checks the facts, you check the voice. Every change I make in revision comes with the reason.

  • Every legal or clinical statement has a linked primary source.
  • Dates and figures are checked against the source on the day of review.
  • A plain disclaimer says what the article is not: legal, insurance or medical advice.
  • In client work, your clinician approves every clinical statement before anything is published.

Want to see how I explain changes? Three full edits, every change with its reason.

06

Final and handoff

Title tag, meta description, internal links and formatting for your CMS, ready to publish.
